The results address four questions: whether readable and answerable findings are owned (Sections~\ref{sec:headline}--\ref{sec:deficit}), whether difficulty explains the gap (\ref{sec:coco}), where the gap arises (\ref{sec:reader}--\ref{sec:attributes}), and which direction the model answers along (\ref{sec:ansdir}); Section~\ref{sec:controls} checks the controls.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:headline}

On both chest datasets, reading and answering pass where writing does not (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, Figures~\ref{fig:teaser} and~\ref{fig:overview}a). Clinical concepts are readable in \cfChestReadablePct\% of the \cfChestReadableN{} chest cells and answerable in \cfChestAnswerablePct\%, but owned in \cfChestOwnedPct\%. Even among the cells that pass both earlier grades, \cfReadAnsOwnedPct\% are owned, against \cfRestOwnedPct\% for the rest. Most chest writes fail at the write itself: \cfChestRefBelow{} of \cfChestCellsN{} label directions stay within the random and sham references, and of the \cfChestRefMet{} that clear them, \cfChestRefStrong{} lose to a competitor. \label{sec:seed}The Effusion case of Section~\ref{sec:introduction} reproduces on 600 new patients ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=\cfSeedEffO$; Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}), and it is typical. Across the grid, Effusion is owned in \cfEffusionOwnedBlocks{} of \cfChestBlocks{} chest blocks, and in \cfEffusionOwnedSmall{} of these $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$.

\subsection{Where the write lands}
\label{sec:deficit}

On chest radiographs the write spreads across findings instead of landing on its own question. The own-question share is \cfOwnShareNih\% on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert, against \cfOwnShareCoco\% on COCO. On the chest sets the own write is small (median $W_{q,q}$ of $\cfMedianWqqNih$ and $\cfMedianWqqChex$, against $\cfMedianWqqCoco$ on COCO). On CheXpert, the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts, and a row of $W$ is flat or peaks off the diagonal; on COCO the diagonal dominates (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}). At every Qwen2.5-VL size a competing direction raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does (Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B).

\paragraph{Single images.} On a radiograph, the Effusion write raises the Effusion answer from $\cfExChestClean$ to $\cfExChestOwn$, and the Nodule write to $\cfExChestComp$ (Figure~\ref{fig:example}). On a natural image, the dog write moves the dog answer from $\cfExCocoClean$ to $\cfExCocoOwn$ and no other answer.

\paragraph{Fragility.} Owned chest cells are fragile. The effect floor keeps \cfChestFloored{} of the \cfChestOwned{}, against \cfCocoFloored{} of \cfCocoOwned{} on COCO, and \cfRefitGradeStableNih{} of \cfRefitGradeOwnedNih{} owned NIH cells survive both refits, against \cfRefitGradeStableCoco{} of \cfRefitGradeOwnedCoco{} on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-refit}).

\subsection{Natural-image and difficulty controls}
\label{sec:coco}

The same procedure owns most natural objects, and the contrast survives two difficulty controls. On COCO, RAW owns \cfCocoOwned{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} cells (\cfCocoOwnedPct\%). Only \cfCocoCompetitor{} cells have a stronger competitor, and \cfCocoBlocksFourPlus{} of the \cfCocoBlocks{} checkpoints own at least four of the six objects. COCO ownership also follows the steering strength, rising from $\cfDoseCocoLow$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $\cfDoseCocoPeak$ at $\alpha=+0.25$, while NIH ownership stays between $\cfDoseNihMin$ and $\cfDoseNihMax$ at every strength (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}b).

Six small or fine-grained COCO categories (handbag, book, backpack, traffic light, knife, and cell phone) are owned in \cfFgFineOwned{} of \cfFgFineCells{} cells, against \cfFgEasySameOwned{} of \cfFgEasySameCells{} for the six easy objects in the same checkpoints (sign test $p=\cfFgSameSignP$), although their median clean-answer AUROC is lower (\cfFgFineAuroc{} against \cfFgEasySameAuroc{}; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-difficulty}). Among the best-answered cells, \cfStratChestTopOwned{} of \cfStratChestTopN{} chest cells are owned (\cfStratChestTopPct\%) against \cfStratNatTopOwned{} of \cfStratNatTopN{} natural ones (\cfStratNatTopPct\%; difference $\cfStratCvnDiff$, 95\% CI $[\cfStratCvnLo,\cfStratCvnHi]$).

\subsection{Same tower, different readers}
\label{sec:reader}

Checkpoints that share a vision tower differ in what they own: Gemma~3 4B, 12B, and 27B share one SigLIP \citep{zhai2023siglip} tower, MedGemma 4B and 27B one MedSigLIP \citep{sellergren2025medgemma} tower, and LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B one CLIP \citep{radford2021clip} tower. Within Gemma~3 and MedGemma the probes and write vectors are identical up to bf16 precision (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}), so only the reader differs. In \cfPairsRatioAboveOne{} of \cfPairsRatioN{} shared-tower pair--dataset combinations the readers differ by more than a refit (median $|\Delta O_q|$ \cfPairsRatioMin{}--\cfPairsRatioMax{} refit standard deviations, up to \cfPairsRatioSingleMax{} for one concept; Figure~\ref{fig:readers}). On NIH Effusion, the same write gives Gemma~3 4B and 12B an $O_q$ gap of $\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelve$. LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own no chest concept but \cfLlavaSevenCoco{} and \cfLlavaThirteenCoco{} COCO objects (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-reader}).

The three checkpoints with chest-trained towers read and answer the findings best (median NIH probe AUROC \cfSpecTowerProbeAuroc{} against \cfSpecRestProbeAuroc{}). They own \cfSpecTowerOwned{} of \cfSpecTowerN{} chest cells, against \cfSpecMedOwned{} of \cfSpecMedN{} for medical and \cfSpecGenOwned{} of \cfSpecGenN{} for general ones. Scale raises chest ownership in some families (none at Qwen2.5-VL 3B, three NIH concepts at 72B), while InternVL3.5 answers every NIH question at every size with writes within $\cfInternvlWqqMax$ of zero (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}).

\subsection{Non-clinical attributes}
\label{sec:attributes}

Non-clinical attributes of the same radiographs fail in the same way as the findings. Projection, sex, and age, written in one family with the findings, are read at least as well (median probe AUROC \cfAttrAurocMin{}--\cfAttrAurocMax{} against \cfAttrClinAuroc{}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-attr}) and owned at the same rate (\cfAttrAllAttrOwned{} of \cfAttrAllAttrN{} attribute cells, \cfAttrAllClinOwned{} of \cfAttrAllClinN{} finding cells). Among the best-answered cells, attributes are owned in \cfStratAttrTopOwned{} of \cfStratAttrTopN{} and findings in \cfStratFindTopOwned{} of \cfStratFindTopN{}.

\subsection{Answer-fitted directions}
\label{sec:ansdir}

A direction fitted to the model's own answers is owned where the label direction is not. The answer direction $a_q$ (Section~\ref{sec:ownership}) is owned in \cfAnsChestOwnedA{} of \cfAnsChestN{} chest cells, against \cfAnsChestOwnedLabel{} for the label direction, and it stays owned in \cfAnsdirtChestKept{} of \cfAnsdirtChestKeptN{} chest (cell, template) pairs under held-out templates (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}).

The answer direction tracks the model rather than the label: its median AUROC is $\cfValAurocAnsCleanChest$ against the model's clean answer and $\cfValAurocAnsChest$ against the report label, the reverse of the probe ($\cfValAurocProbeCleanChest$ and $\cfValAurocProbeChest$; Table~\ref{tab:cf-validation}). The two directions are nearly orthogonal on chest radiographs (median cosine $\cfAnsCosChestMed$) and aligned on natural images ($\cfAnsCosCocoMed$). The separation persists at four endpoints fitted to neither direction: the negated question, a forced choice in both orders, and the finding word in a report continuation. There the answer direction is owned in \cfSemAnswerOwned{} of \cfSemCells{} cells and the label direction in \cfSemLabelOwned{} (Table~\ref{tab:cf-semend}).

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}
\label{sec:robust-geometry}

The grade holds under every control below.

\paragraph{References and directions.} Owned writes rank 1--6 among their own and the 119 random writes (Figure~\ref{fig:rank}). Four alternative directions, the class-mean difference, the Haufe pattern \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, and normals orthogonalized or residualized against the other five concepts \citep{nicolson2025explaining}, are owned in \cfAltdirChestPctMin{}--\cfAltdirChestPctMax\% of a chest dataset's cells against \cfAltdirCocoPctMin{}--\cfAltdirCocoPctMax\% on COCO, and token-concentrated writes are owned no more often than uniform ones (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}). The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction or most co-occurring label only at chance (Table~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}).

\paragraph{Labels and scoring.} Directions graded on \cfValidRows{} CheXpert films labeled by radiologist consensus reproduce the owned grade in \cfValidOwnedAgree{} of \cfValidCells{} cells, and directions refitted on those labels are owned in \cfValidfitOwnedExpert{} of \cfValidfitCells{} cells, against \cfValidfitOwnedReport{} for the report-label directions. Adding every extra dataset label as a competitor changes the owned count by at most one cell (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}). The grade also holds under the connector site, the logit scale, unsaturated rows, and fp32 rescoring (Appendices~\ref{app:cf-ledger}--\ref{app:cf-robustness}).
