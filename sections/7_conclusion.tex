\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation does not make its probe direction a handle on that concept. A steering claim therefore needs the competitor comparison, not a random-direction control alone: a write that clears random and sham directions can still be the write of another finding. Checkpoints that write identical vectors own different concepts, so ownership belongs to the checkpoint that will be deployed rather than to the vision tower it contains. The direction that does control the answer is fitted to the model's answers rather than to the label, and on chest radiographs it is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction. Readability is a property of a classifier fitted to the representation; control is a property of a direction and the model that reads it.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The grades describe linear writes at two sites, the selected vision layer and the connector output, at one primary steering strength with a five-point sweep; deeper language-model layers and nonlinear edits may behave differently. Each dataset contributes six concepts, and the chest labels are report-derived, with radiologist labels available for a CheXpert validation subset. The grade needs a discrete answer. MAIRA-2 \citep{bannur2024maira} emits reports rather than answers and fails the image-free mapping check on every template (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-gates}), and the discrete-diffusion report generator of \citet{chen2026echo} has the same shape, so both fall outside the evaluation.

Ownership grades the write comparison; it does not predict effect size. On the four semantic endpoints, adding the ownership contrast to write magnitude, probe selectivity, and clean-answer AUROC adds no held-out predictive value beyond a permutation null ($p=\cfSemCvLocoP$ and $p=\cfSemCvLoboP$ under the concept and block splits; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-ansdir}). The answer direction is fitted on training rows to the model's own clean answers and written on test rows and held-out templates, so it is a handle on the model's answer. Its median AUROC against the report label is $\cfValAurocAnsChest$, below the probe's $\cfValAurocProbeChest$, so it controls the answer without reading the finding better.
