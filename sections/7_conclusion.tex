\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation does not make its probe direction a handle on that concept. A steering claim therefore needs the competitor comparison, because a write that clears random and sham directions can still be the write of another finding. Checkpoints that write the same vectors, up to bf16 precision, own different concepts, so ownership belongs to the checkpoint that will be deployed rather than to the vision tower it contains. The direction that does control the answer is fitted to the model's answers rather than to the label, and on chest radiographs it is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction. Readability is a property of a classifier fitted to the representation; control is a property of a direction and the model that reads it.
