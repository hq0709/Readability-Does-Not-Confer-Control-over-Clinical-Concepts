Vision-language models (VLMs) now answer questions about images across domains, from everyday photographs to medical scans \citep{liu2024improved,bai2025qwen25vl,li2023llavamed,sellergren2025medgemma}, yet how they reach an answer remains largely opaque. Interpretability methods aim to open this process concept by concept. Linear probes test which concepts a model represents \citep{alain2017understanding,belinkov2022probing}, and activation steering adds a probe direction back to the representation to change the model's behavior \citep{turner2023steering,panickssery2024caa}, including in VLMs \citep{rajaram2025lineofsight,ferrando2026explain}. Used together, they presuppose that a concept decodable along a direction can also be controlled along it. The presupposition matters most in high-stakes domains such as medical imaging, where probes already decode findings such as pleural effusion (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}; \citealp{zhu2026lost,pedersen2026probes}) and a handle on each would let a model be audited and corrected finding by finding. Whether a decodable finding can also be controlled along its probe direction remains open.

A case in Qwen2.5-VL-7B \citep{bai2025qwen25vl} on NIH ChestX-ray14 \citep{wang2017chestxray} shows why it matters (Figure~\ref{fig:framework}b). Adding the Effusion probe direction to the visual representation raises the model's probability of answering yes to ``Is there pleural effusion?'' by $\cfSeedWEff$, above the random and sham references. By common steering criteria, the probe direction controls effusion. However, adding the nodule direction to the same patients raises the effusion answer by $\cfSeedWNodule$. The effusion direction is not specific to effusion.

Prior work questions this presupposition: information a probe recovers need not be used by the model \citep{ravichander2021probing}, separability-trained vectors absorb distractor signals \citep{pahde2025navigating}, and the features that identify a concept differ from those controlling the output \citep{arad2025saes}. None compares a probe direction with the directions of competing concepts, and random and shuffled (sham) directions carry no label.

Testing the presupposition requires a labeled reference, namely the directions of the other concepts. We call a concept \emph{owned} when its own direction, rather than one fitted in the same manner for a competing concept, controls its answer. We formalize this comparison as RAW (Read--Answer--Write; Section~\ref{sec:framework}), an evaluation that grades each concept as readable when its probe beats random-label probes, answerable when the model's unsteered answer beats chance, and owned by its direction. Each dataset has six concepts; a checkpoint--concept pair is a \emph{cell}. Because RAW requires only a labeled concept set and an open-weight VLM, it applies unchanged to natural images, which serve as a control. We evaluate \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints from \cfFamiliesWord{} families (3B--72B) on NIH ChestX-ray14, CheXpert Plus \citep{chambon2024chexpertplus}, and COCO \citep{lin2014coco}.

The effusion example is representative. Chest findings pass the read and answer grades about as often as natural objects, yet only \cfChestOwnedPct\% of chest cells are owned, against \cfCocoOwnedPct\% of object cells. The gap holds across families and scales, among the concepts each model answers best, and for directions orthogonalized against the other findings. The deficit also extends beyond clinical findings to non-clinical attributes of the same radiographs, and medical checkpoints, including three with chest-trained vision towers, leave most findings unowned. COCO objects that the models answer less accurately remain owned. Checkpoints with identical vision towers own different findings, which points to the connector and language model that read the representation. A direction fitted to the model's own answers is owned more often and, on chest radiographs only, is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction. Steering claims built on probe directions thus need the competitor comparison.

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item We identify a gap between reading and controlling concepts on chest radiographs: readable, answerable findings are rarely owned by their probe directions, unlike natural objects.
\item We introduce RAW, an evaluation that separates reading a concept from writing it by scoring each probe direction against directions fitted the same way for the competing concepts, and release it with scorecards and write matrices.
\item Across \cfCells{} cells and about 30 million scored answers, controlling for difficulty, direction, write site, and label source, we trace the deficit to the connector and language model, which answer along a different direction.
\end{itemize}
