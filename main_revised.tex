\begin{abstract}
Vision-language models (VLMs) answer questions about images from everyday photographs to medical scans, and interpretability aims to audit them concept by concept: a probe decodes a concept along a direction, and steering adds that direction back. Does decodability along a direction imply control along it? On chest radiographs, adding the nodule direction can raise the model's effusion answer more than adding the effusion direction does, which standard checks against unlabeled random directions miss. We introduce RAW (Read--Answer--Write), an evaluation that grades each concept as readable by a probe, answerable by the model, and \emph{owned} when its own direction, rather than a competing concept's, controls its answer. Across \cfCheckpoints{} general-purpose and medical checkpoints, findings on NIH ChestX-ray14 and CheXpert Plus are readable and answerable, yet only \cfChestOwnedPct{}\% are owned across checkpoints, against \cfCocoOwnedPct{}\% for COCO objects. The gap persists among the best-answered concepts, under alternative directions and expert labels, and for non-clinical radiograph attributes. Checkpoints with identical vision towers own different concepts, pointing to the connector and language model, and a direction fitted to the model's answers is owned more often yet nearly orthogonal to the probe direction on chest radiographs. Decodability is not enough for control; steering claims need the competitor comparison.

\end{abstract}

Vision-language models (VLMs) now answer questions about images across domains, from everyday photographs to medical scans \citep{liu2024improved,bai2025qwen25vl,li2023llavamed,sellergren2025medgemma}, yet how they reach an answer remains poorly understood. Interpretability methods aim to open this process concept by concept. Linear probes test which concepts a model represents \citep{alain2017understanding,belinkov2022probing}, and activation steering adds a probe direction back to the representation to change the model's behavior \citep{turner2023steering,panickssery2024caa}, including in VLMs \citep{rajaram2025lineofsight,ferrando2026explain}. Used together, they assume that a concept decodable along a direction can also be controlled along it. The assumption matters most in high-stakes domains such as medical imaging, where probes already decode clinical findings (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}; \citealp{zhu2026lost,pedersen2026probes}) and a handle on each would let a model be audited and corrected finding by finding. Whether a decodable finding can also be controlled along its probe direction remains open.

A case in Qwen2.5-VL-7B \citep{bai2025qwen25vl} on NIH ChestX-ray14 \citep{wang2017chestxray} shows why it matters (Figure~\ref{fig:framework}b). Adding the Effusion probe direction to the visual representation raises the model's probability of answering yes to ``Is there pleural effusion?'' by $\cfSeedWEff$, above random and shuffled directions. By common steering criteria, the probe direction controls effusion. However, adding the lung-nodule direction to the same patients raises the effusion answer by $\cfSeedWNodule$. The effusion direction is thus not specific to effusion.

Prior work questions this assumption: information a probe recovers need not be used by the model \citep{ravichander2021probing}, separability-trained vectors absorb distractor signals \citep{pahde2025navigating}, and the features that identify a concept differ from those controlling the output \citep{arad2025saes}. None compares a probe direction with the directions of competing concepts, and steering evaluations compare it only with random and shuffled directions, which carry no label.

Testing the assumption requires a labeled reference: the directions of the other concepts. We call a concept \emph{owned} when its own direction, rather than one fitted in the same manner for a competing concept, controls its answer. We formalize this comparison as RAW (Read--Answer--Write; Section~\ref{sec:framework}), an evaluation that grades each concept as readable when its probe beats random-label probes, answerable when the model's unsteered answer beats chance, and owned by its direction. Because RAW requires only a labeled concept set and an open-weight VLM, it applies unchanged to natural images, which serve as a control for the method. We evaluate \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints from \cfFamiliesWord{} families (3B--72B) on six concepts each from NIH ChestX-ray14, CheXpert Plus \citep{chambon2024chexpertplus}, and COCO \citep{lin2014coco}, grading every checkpoint--concept pair, or \emph{cell}.

The effusion example is representative. Chest findings pass the read and answer grades about as often as natural objects, yet only \cfChestOwnedPct\% of chest cells are owned, against \cfCocoOwnedPct\% of object cells. The gap holds across families and scales, including medical checkpoints with chest-trained vision towers, among the concepts each model answers best, and for directions orthogonalized against the other findings. The deficit also extends beyond clinical findings to non-clinical attributes of the same radiographs. Nor does difficulty explain it: COCO objects the models answer less accurately remain owned. Checkpoints with identical vision towers own different findings, which points to the connector and language model downstream of the tower. These respond to a different direction: one fitted to the model's own answers is owned more often and, on chest radiographs only, is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction. Steering claims built on probe directions thus need the competitor comparison.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item We identify a gap between reading and controlling concepts on chest radiographs: readable, answerable findings are rarely owned by their probe directions, unlike natural objects.
\item We introduce RAW, an evaluation for any open-weight VLM that tests whether a probe direction controls its own concept or a competing one.
\item Across \cfCells{} cells and about 30 million scored answers, controlling for difficulty, direction, write site, and label source, we trace the deficit to the connector and language model, which answer along a direction nearly orthogonal to the probe direction on chest radiographs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Probing and its controls.}
Probes track information across depth \citep{alain2017understanding}. A probe score reflects the learner as well as the representation, which motivates control tasks \citep{hewitt2019designing}, and our readability grade adopts them. Erasure tests use by deleting a concept \citep{ravfogel2020null,elazar2021amnesic}, in closed form for every linear reader \citep{belrose2023leace}. RAW asks a complementary question: whether the concept's own direction is the one that moves its answer. In medical VLMs, probes map classification information across towers, connectors, and language layers \citep{zhu2026lost}, and they expose acquisition and device shortcuts in MedCLIP \citep{wang2022medclip,pedersen2026probes}. We intervene on the clinical direction at the site where it is read.

\paragraph{Concept directions and steering.}
A concept activation vector is a classifier normal that grades a class by the directional derivative along it \citep{kim2018tcav}. Such a normal shifts with its layer, entangles with correlated concepts, depends on its negative set, and carries where the concept was read \citep{nicolson2025explaining}. Representation engineering and activation steering write a concept back at inference time \citep{zou2023representation,turner2023steering,panickssery2024caa}. Classifier weights act as a filter rather than the pattern they detect \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, and mass-mean directions steer truth judgments better than logistic normals \citep{marks2024geometry}. Benchmarks score detection and steering separately \citep{wu2025axbench}, and alignment search certifies a subspace as causal when it implements an interpretable variable \citep{geiger2024alignments}. These works judge a direction against its own concept. Ownership judges it against directions fitted the same way for the other concepts, and Section~\ref{sec:robust-geometry} runs alternative constructions, a second site, and token-concentrated writes through RAW.

\paragraph{Writing into a vision-language model.}
Image features are linearly decodable in a VLM's hidden layers, and editing them changes the model's answers \citep{rajaram2025lineofsight}. Steering a sparse-autoencoder feature of the vision tower and asking the language model what it sees recovers what the feature encodes \citep{ferrando2026explain}. Causal probing writes difference-in-means vectors into the language model's residual stream and scores each concept against its own effect \citep{deng2026causal}. We start instead from a clinical label, write the representation the language model consumes, and score each concept against its competitors.

\section{Method: Measuring Ownership}
\label{sec:framework}

RAW asks three questions about a concept $c$ in a checkpoint $M$ on a dataset with binary labels: whether $c$ is readable from the visual representation the language model consumes, whether $M$ answers the question about $c$, and whether writing $c$'s own direction moves $c$'s answer more than any alternative. A registered protocol fixes an estimator and a decision rule for each question (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-labels}).

\subsection{Reading and writing at the selected vision layer}
\label{sec:site}

Every VLM in the grid passes an image through a vision tower and hands a specific tensor to a connector or merger that produces the language model's inputs. The \emph{selected vision layer} is the last visual block whose output that consumer reads, restricted to the token positions the consumer actually uses (CLS or padding rows are excluded where the consumer drops them). The \emph{connector site} is the consumer's output. Appendix~\ref{app:repro-sites} identifies the block for every family. For image $i$ and consumed token $t$, let $\hvec_{it}\in\R^D$ be the output of the selected vision layer, where $D$ is the tower's hidden width; mean pooling over the consumed tokens gives the read vector $\hvec_i$. A write adds the unit direction $\hat\wvec_c$ of concept $c$ (Section~\ref{sec:directions}) to the same tokens,
\begin{equation}
\hvec'_{it}=\hvec_{it}+\alpha\,\lVert\hvec_{it}\rVert_2\,\hat\wvec_c,
\label{eq:intervention}
\end{equation}
where the steering strength $\alpha$ is a fraction of each token's own norm. Scaling by the norm keeps $\alpha$ comparable across families whose activation scales differ by orders of magnitude, and the sign of $\alpha$ selects the concept's positive or negative pole. Reading and writing therefore use the same representation and the same positions. The connector and the language model, everything downstream of the selected vision layer, form the \emph{reader} of that representation.

\subsection{Label directions and controls}
\label{sec:directions}

The probe that reads a concept also supplies the direction that writes it. Each concept is read by a linear probe on a shared 512-dimensional view of the representation. A fixed Gaussian matrix $R\in\R^{D\times512}$ maps the read vector to features $\boldsymbol x_i=R^{\top}\hvec_i$, which are standardized with training-row means and scales $\boldsymbol s$. An $\ell_2$-regularized logistic regression with coefficient vector $\boldsymbol\beta_c$ then scores concept $c$. The regression uses inverse regularization strength $C=1$ and at most 2{,}000 iterations, and is fitted with three seeds. The probe's logit is thus an affine function of $\hvec_i$ whose gradient is $R\,\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol s)^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_c$, and the write direction is that gradient at unit length,
\begin{equation}
\hat\wvec_c=\frac{\boldsymbol g_c}{\lVert\boldsymbol g_c\rVert_2},\qquad \boldsymbol g_c=R\,\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol s)^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta_c .
\label{eq:lift}
\end{equation}
The vector $\hat\wvec_c$ is the direction in the consumed representation along which the probe's evidence for $c$ grows fastest (Appendix~\ref{app:repro-probe} gives the numerical details). We call it the concept's \emph{label direction}, since it is fitted to the concept's labels.

Two families of controls bracket the label direction. For reading, 20 control probes are fitted to random labels drawn within recurring image types (view, sex, and age decade on chest sets; aspect and area buckets on COCO), so that they have the capacity and the nuisance structure of the concept probe. With $y$ the concept labels and $r$ the probe's scores on the calibration rows, and $y^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_k$ and $r^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_k$ the random labels and scores of control probe $k$, the probe's selectivity is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S={}&\operatorname{AUROC}(y,r)\\
&-\tfrac{1}{20}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{20}\operatorname{AUROC}(y^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_k,r^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_k),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:selectivity}
\end{equation}
which is positive when the probe reads its concept better than a probe of equal capacity reads an arbitrary label with the same nuisance structure. For writing, the \emph{reference family} contains 119 random unit directions, a coordinate-permutation sham of $\hat\wvec_c$ (same coordinates, scrambled arrangement), and the five label directions of the other clinical concepts, all written at the same steering strength to the same images.

\subsection{The ownership margin}
\label{sec:ownership}

Each dataset has six concepts, and we index both the yes/no question about a concept and the direction fitted for it by that concept. For question $q$ and direction $d$, $W_{q,d}$ is the mean over test images of the change in $P(\mathrm{yes})$ for question $q$ when $\hat\wvec_d$ is written, each image scored with and without the write. Here $P(\mathrm{yes})=\sigma(\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}})$, where $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}$ and $\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ are the log-probabilities of the two answers at the first answer position. (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Row $q$ of the $6\times6$ write matrix $W$ records how far each direction moves question $q$, and its diagonal entry $W_{q,q}$ is the concept's own write (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}). Ownership is the diagonal margin
\begin{equation}
O_q=W_{q,q}-\max_{d\neq q}W_{q,d},
\label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
the advantage of the concept's own direction over its strongest clinical competitor on the concept's own question. A large $W_{q,q}$ shows that the direction reaches the answer; only $O_q>0$ shows that the label names the effective direction. A concept can therefore fail ownership in two ways: its own write stays within the random and sham references, or the write clears them without holding its advantage over every competitor. The case in Section~\ref{sec:introduction} is of the second kind.

As a diagnostic, we also fit the unsteered answer margin $\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ by ridge regression on the training features and lift the coefficients with Equation~\ref{eq:lift}; the resulting \emph{answer direction} $a_q$, a control vector in the sense of \citet{zou2023representation}, is written and graded like a label direction.

Table~\ref{tab:grades} states the three grades. Readable and answerable are computed on the calibration rows, which carry no write, and need at least ten known positives and ten known negatives there; the write matrix and every ownership quantity are computed on the test rows, which share no patient with the calibration rows. The \emph{clean answer} is $P(\mathrm{yes})$ with no write. The \emph{steering reference} requires $W_{q,q}>0$, above the 95th percentile of the 119 random writes and above the sham's absolute effect. Together with $O_q>0$, it means that the concept's own direction moves its own answer further than any of the five competitors does. A cell that meets the reference while some competitor moves the answer at least as far has a \emph{stronger competitor}.

A checkpoint--concept pair on one dataset is a \emph{cell}, and a checkpoint--dataset pair is a \emph{block}. An alternative direction is owned in a cell when its write meets the same rule for that concept. A block contributes to the answerable and owned counts only when its write matrix and its calibration pass both completed. \paragraph{Checkpoints.}
We evaluate \cfCheckpoints{} instruction-tuned checkpoints, 3B to 72B, from \cfFamiliesWord{} families. Six families are general-purpose: Qwen2.5-VL \citep{bai2025qwen25vl}, Qwen3-VL \citep{qwen2025qwen3vl}, InternVL3.5 \citep{wang2025internvl35}, Gemma~3 \citep{gemma2025gemma3}, LLaVA-1.5 \citep{liu2024improved}, and Llama~3.2~Vision \citep{meta2024llama32vision}. Six are medical: MedGemma \citep{sellergren2025medgemma}, Lingshu \citep{lasa2025lingshu}, LLaVA-Med \citep{li2023llavamed}, CheXagent \citep{chen2024chexagent}, HuatuoGPT-Vision \citep{chen2024huatuogptvision}, and LLaVA-Rad \citep{zambranochaves2025llavarad}. Three of them, CheXagent-2-3B, CheXagent-8B, and LLaVA-Rad-7B, have vision towers trained on chest radiographs (Table~\ref{tab:cf-models}; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-grid}).

\paragraph{Datasets and cohorts.}
NIH ChestX-ray14 \citep{wang2017chestxray} and CheXpert Plus \citep{chambon2024chexpertplus} supply frontal radiographs with report-derived labels \citep{irvin2019chexpert} for six findings each, and COCO \citep{lin2014coco} supplies six object categories as a natural-image control (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-labels}). One fixed cohort per dataset, shared by every checkpoint and disjoint across roles, holds 20{,}000 training rows for the probes and the answer direction, 400 calibration rows for the readable and answerable grades, and 600 test rows for every write (Appendix~\ref{app:repro-cohorts}).

\paragraph{Questions and scale.}
Each concept is asked as a yes/no question, with five further templates as held-out wordings (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Writes use $\alpha=+0.25$, with a sweep over five further strengths. The campaign comprises \cfBlocks{} blocks, \cfCells{} scored concept cells, about 30 million scored answers, and roughly 3{,}000 A100-hours (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-coverage}).

\paragraph{Measures.}
A model \emph{answers best} on a cell when its clean-answer AUROC is at least \cfStratTopEdge{}. The \emph{own-question share} is the fraction of the positive answer change, $\max(W_{q,d},0)$ averaged over checkpoints, that falls on the written concept's own question. Reader differences are measured in \emph{refit standard deviations} of $O_q$ across the three probe fits, and an \emph{effect floor} of $0.05$ on $W_{q,q}$ and $O_q$ sets a minimum effect size.

The results address four questions: whether readable and answerable findings are owned (Sections~\ref{sec:headline}--\ref{sec:deficit}), whether difficulty explains the gap (\ref{sec:coco}), where the gap arises (\ref{sec:reader}--\ref{sec:attributes}), and which direction the model answers along (\ref{sec:ansdir}); Section~\ref{sec:controls} checks the controls.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:headline}

On both chest datasets, reading and answering pass where writing does not (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, Figures~\ref{fig:teaser} and~\ref{fig:overview}a). Clinical concepts are readable in \cfChestReadablePct\% of the \cfChestReadableN{} chest cells and answerable in \cfChestAnswerablePct\%, but owned in \cfChestOwnedPct\%. Even among the cells that pass both earlier grades, \cfReadAnsOwnedPct\% are owned, against \cfRestOwnedPct\% for the rest. Most chest writes fail at the write itself: \cfChestRefBelow{} of \cfChestCellsN{} label directions stay within the random and sham references, and of the \cfChestRefMet{} that clear them, \cfChestRefStrong{} lose to a competitor. \label{sec:seed}The Effusion case of Section~\ref{sec:introduction} reproduces on 600 new patients ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=\cfSeedEffO$; Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}), and it is typical. Across the grid, Effusion is owned in \cfEffusionOwnedBlocks{} of \cfChestBlocks{} chest blocks, and in \cfEffusionOwnedSmall{} of these $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$.

\subsection{Where the write lands}
\label{sec:deficit}

On chest radiographs the write spreads across findings instead of landing on its own question. The own-question share is \cfOwnShareNih\% on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert, against \cfOwnShareCoco\% on COCO. On the chest sets the own write is small (median $W_{q,q}$ of $\cfMedianWqqNih$ and $\cfMedianWqqChex$, against $\cfMedianWqqCoco$ on COCO). On CheXpert, the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts, and a row of $W$ is flat or peaks off the diagonal; on COCO the diagonal dominates (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}). At every Qwen2.5-VL size a competing direction raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does (Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B).

\paragraph{Single images.} On a radiograph, the Effusion write raises the Effusion answer from $\cfExChestClean$ to $\cfExChestOwn$, and the Nodule write to $\cfExChestComp$ (Figure~\ref{fig:example}). On a natural image, the dog write moves the dog answer from $\cfExCocoClean$ to $\cfExCocoOwn$ and no other answer.

\paragraph{Fragility.} Owned chest cells are fragile. The effect floor keeps \cfChestFloored{} of the \cfChestOwned{}, against \cfCocoFloored{} of \cfCocoOwned{} on COCO, and \cfRefitGradeStableNih{} of \cfRefitGradeOwnedNih{} owned NIH cells survive both refits, against \cfRefitGradeStableCoco{} of \cfRefitGradeOwnedCoco{} on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-refit}).

\subsection{Natural-image and difficulty controls}
\label{sec:coco}

The same procedure owns most natural objects, and the contrast survives two difficulty controls. On COCO, RAW owns \cfCocoOwned{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} cells (\cfCocoOwnedPct\%). Only \cfCocoCompetitor{} cells have a stronger competitor, and \cfCocoBlocksFourPlus{} of the \cfCocoBlocks{} checkpoints own at least four of the six objects. COCO ownership also follows the steering strength, rising from $\cfDoseCocoLow$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $\cfDoseCocoPeak$ at $\alpha=+0.25$, while NIH ownership stays between $\cfDoseNihMin$ and $\cfDoseNihMax$ at every strength (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}b).

Six small or fine-grained COCO categories (handbag, book, backpack, traffic light, knife, and cell phone) are owned in \cfFgFineOwned{} of \cfFgFineCells{} cells, against \cfFgEasySameOwned{} of \cfFgEasySameCells{} for the six easy objects in the same checkpoints (sign test $p=\cfFgSameSignP$), although their median clean-answer AUROC is lower (\cfFgFineAuroc{} against \cfFgEasySameAuroc{}; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-difficulty}). Among the best-answered cells, \cfStratChestTopOwned{} of \cfStratChestTopN{} chest cells are owned (\cfStratChestTopPct\%) against \cfStratNatTopOwned{} of \cfStratNatTopN{} natural ones (\cfStratNatTopPct\%; difference $\cfStratCvnDiff$, 95\% CI $[\cfStratCvnLo,\cfStratCvnHi]$).

\subsection{Same tower, different readers}
\label{sec:reader}

Checkpoints that share a vision tower differ in what they own: Gemma~3 4B, 12B, and 27B share one SigLIP \citep{zhai2023siglip} tower, MedGemma 4B and 27B one MedSigLIP \citep{sellergren2025medgemma} tower, and LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B one CLIP \citep{radford2021clip} tower. Within Gemma~3 and MedGemma the probes and write vectors are identical up to bf16 precision (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}), so only the reader differs. In \cfPairsRatioAboveOne{} of \cfPairsRatioN{} shared-tower pair--dataset combinations the readers differ by more than a refit (median $|\Delta O_q|$ \cfPairsRatioMin{}--\cfPairsRatioMax{} refit standard deviations, up to \cfPairsRatioSingleMax{} for one concept; Figure~\ref{fig:readers}). On NIH Effusion, the same write gives Gemma~3 4B and 12B an $O_q$ gap of $\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelve$. LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own no chest concept but \cfLlavaSevenCoco{} and \cfLlavaThirteenCoco{} COCO objects (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-reader}).

The three checkpoints with chest-trained towers read and answer the findings best (median NIH probe AUROC \cfSpecTowerProbeAuroc{} against \cfSpecRestProbeAuroc{}). They own \cfSpecTowerOwned{} of \cfSpecTowerN{} chest cells, against \cfSpecMedOwned{} of \cfSpecMedN{} for medical and \cfSpecGenOwned{} of \cfSpecGenN{} for general ones. Scale raises chest ownership in some families (none at Qwen2.5-VL 3B, three NIH concepts at 72B), while InternVL3.5 answers every NIH question at every size with writes within $\cfInternvlWqqMax$ of zero (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}).

\subsection{Non-clinical attributes}
\label{sec:attributes}

Non-clinical attributes of the same radiographs fail in the same way as the findings. Projection, sex, and age, written in one family with the findings, are read at least as well (median probe AUROC \cfAttrAurocMin{}--\cfAttrAurocMax{} against \cfAttrClinAuroc{}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-attr}) and owned at the same rate (\cfAttrAllAttrOwned{} of \cfAttrAllAttrN{} attribute cells, \cfAttrAllClinOwned{} of \cfAttrAllClinN{} finding cells). Among the best-answered cells, attributes are owned in \cfStratAttrTopOwned{} of \cfStratAttrTopN{} and findings in \cfStratFindTopOwned{} of \cfStratFindTopN{}.

\subsection{Answer-fitted directions}
\label{sec:ansdir}

A direction fitted to the model's own answers is owned where the label direction is not. The answer direction $a_q$ (Section~\ref{sec:ownership}) is owned in \cfAnsChestOwnedA{} of \cfAnsChestN{} chest cells, against \cfAnsChestOwnedLabel{} for the label direction, and it stays owned in \cfAnsdirtChestKept{} of \cfAnsdirtChestKeptN{} chest (cell, template) pairs under held-out templates (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}).

The answer direction tracks the model rather than the label: its median AUROC is $\cfValAurocAnsCleanChest$ against the model's clean answer and $\cfValAurocAnsChest$ against the report label, the reverse of the probe ($\cfValAurocProbeCleanChest$ and $\cfValAurocProbeChest$; Table~\ref{tab:cf-validation}). The two directions are nearly orthogonal on chest radiographs (median cosine $\cfAnsCosChestMed$) and aligned on natural images ($\cfAnsCosCocoMed$). The separation persists at four endpoints fitted to neither direction: the negated question, a forced choice in both orders, and the finding word in a report continuation. There the answer direction is owned in \cfSemAnswerOwned{} of \cfSemCells{} cells and the label direction in \cfSemLabelOwned{} (Table~\ref{tab:cf-semend}).

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}
\label{sec:robust-geometry}

The grade holds under every control below.

\paragraph{References and directions.} Owned writes rank 1--6 among their own and the 119 random writes (Figure~\ref{fig:rank}). Four alternative directions, the class-mean difference, the Haufe pattern \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, and normals orthogonalized or residualized against the other five concepts \citep{nicolson2025explaining}, are owned in \cfAltdirChestPctMin{}--\cfAltdirChestPctMax\% of a chest dataset's cells against \cfAltdirCocoPctMin{}--\cfAltdirCocoPctMax\% on COCO, and token-concentrated writes are owned no more often than uniform ones (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}). The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction or most co-occurring label only at chance (Table~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}).

\paragraph{Labels and scoring.} Directions graded on \cfValidRows{} CheXpert films labeled by radiologist consensus reproduce the owned grade in \cfValidOwnedAgree{} of \cfValidCells{} cells, and directions refitted on those labels are owned in \cfValidfitOwnedExpert{} of \cfValidfitCells{} cells, against \cfValidfitOwnedReport{} for the report-label directions. Adding every extra dataset label as a competitor changes the owned count by at most one cell (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}). The grade also holds under the connector site, the logit scale, unsaturated rows, and fp32 rescoring (Appendices~\ref{app:cf-ledger}--\ref{app:cf-robustness}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:implications}

\paragraph{Readability belongs to the probe, ownership to the reader.}
Checkpoints that share a vision tower, and with it the probes and write vectors, own different concepts, and the chest-trained towers that read findings best own \cfSpecTowerOwned{} of \cfSpecTowerN{} chest cells. Ownership therefore belongs on the scorecard of the deployed checkpoint, whose reader decides it.

\paragraph{On chest radiographs, the label direction and the answer direction come apart.}
On natural images the label direction and the model's answer direction are aligned, and writing the label direction controls the answer. On chest radiographs the two are nearly orthogonal, and the answer direction is owned in \cfAnsChestOwnedA{} of \cfAnsChestN{} chest cells where the label direction is owned in \cfAnsChestOwnedLabel{}. The flat rows of the chest write matrices fit a reader that responds to a signal shared across findings \citep{pahde2025navigating}, and identifying that signal is the natural next step. That search should not start from the findings. Projection, sex, and age are read better and owned at the same rate (\cfAttrAllAttrOwned{} of \cfAttrAllAttrN{} attribute cells against \cfAttrAllClinOwned{} of \cfAttrAllClinN{} finding cells; Section~\ref{sec:attributes}), so the signal the reader follows is shared by concepts with no pathology in common. On chest radiographs, the reader chooses its answer along a direction the label does not mark.

\paragraph{What a steering evaluation should report.}
Probe accuracy and answer accuracy, the two quantities most often reported for medical VLMs, leave open whether a write is specific to the concept it names. Random and sham writes show that a direction reaches the answer; directions fitted the same way for the competing findings, written at the same site and steering strength in the same patients, show whether it belongs to its label. Studies that use probe normals as interventions should report the write matrix $W$, compare $O_q$ across layers, strengths, and models, and pair every clinical write with a natural-image control, where RAW does produce ownership.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation does not make its probe direction a handle on that concept. A steering claim therefore needs the competitor comparison, because a write that clears random and sham directions can still be the write of another finding. Checkpoints that write the same vectors, up to bf16 precision, own different concepts, so ownership belongs to the checkpoint that will be deployed rather than to the vision tower it contains. The direction that does control the answer is fitted to the model's answers rather than to the label, and on chest radiographs it is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction. Readability is a property of a classifier fitted to the representation; control is a property of a direction and the model that reads it.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
RAW grades open-weight checkpoints, since the write needs activation access: the \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints span \cfFamiliesWord{} families from 3B to 72B, and API-served models are outside the campaign. The medical modality is chest radiography, with COCO as the natural-image control; CT, pathology, and dermatology remain to be graded. The write is a rank-one additive edit at two sites, the selected vision layer and the connector output, over a grid of steering strengths. Deeper language-model layers, attention-head writes, weight edits, and prompt-level interventions may behave differently. Each dataset contributes six concepts, and the chest labels come from reports, with a radiologist-consensus re-grade on \cfValidRows{} CheXpert films as the check on that label source (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-robustness}).

The grade needs a discrete answer read at one answer position, so it covers yes/no questions and leaves generated report text ungraded. MAIRA-2 \citep{bannur2024maira} emits reports rather than answers and fails the image-free mapping check on every template (Appendix~\ref{app:cf-gates}), and the discrete-diffusion report generator of \citet{chen2026echo} has the same shape.

Ownership grades the write comparison rather than effect size: on the four semantic endpoints, adding the ownership contrast to write magnitude, probe selectivity, and clean-answer AUROC adds no held-out predictive value beyond a permutation null ($p=\cfSemCvLocoP$ and $p=\cfSemCvLoboP$ under the concept and block splits; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-ansdir}). The answer direction is fitted to the model's own clean answers and is a handle on that answer; against the report label its median AUROC is $\cfValAurocAnsChest$, against $\cfValAurocProbeChest$ for the probe.

\section*{Reproducibility statement}
The registered protocol file fixes the sites, projection, probe settings, steering strength grid, reference family, templates, modules, gates, and decision rules. The release contains the RAW code and adapters, the protocol file, the cohort manifests with their seeds, the fitted directions, the per-image outcomes of every module, every block's scorecard and coverage file, the rendered prompts; every table and figure in the paper is regenerated from those files by the scripts in the release. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} states the selected vision layer of every family, the consumed-token mask, the projection and probe construction, the control sampling procedures, the cohort roles, and the protocol amendments with their dates.

The study uses three public datasets under their published terms: NIH ChestX-ray14 (NIH Clinical Center), CheXpert Plus (Stanford AIMI, accessed through its data-use agreement), and COCO (annotations CC BY 4.0). All radiographs are de-identified by their providers; no protected health information was accessed, and no image or report is redistributed. RAW evaluates model behavior under activation edits and is not a diagnostic tool; its grades describe what a probe direction does inside a model and carry no claim of clinical benefit.

No patient and no image appears in two roles of a dataset, and every cohort is disjoint from every pilot-study cohort. Three cohorts are scored. The sites, projection and probe settings, steering strength, reference family, six templates, module set, seven gates, and three decision rules match the registered protocol file. We registered the pilot study's crossover and confirmation analyses before running them (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). After completing the grid, we added the leaderboard, write-flow share, and per-image examples as descriptive summaries of these outputs.

The release also provides per-image outcomes for every module (write, steering strength, refit, site, and template rows), scorecards and coverage files for every block, and rendered prompts. \subsection{Acceptance decisions}
\label{app:cf-gates}

Before a block is scored, seven checks run on the 16 preflight rows at both sites. They are (A) bitwise determinism; (B) identity at $\alpha=0$; (C) reach, which requires the write to change the consumer's input and answer logits and nothing outside the consumed tokens; (D) batched-versus-single agreement of the answer logits within $0.25$; (E) image-free semantic mapping of every template; (F) throughput; and (G) fp32 recomputation of the answer logits. Deviations in (D) are declared and neutralized by scoring every condition of a question in one fixed batch composition. Templates that fail (E) are ineligible. A checkpoint whose yes/no template fails is graded on its eligible B-positive template (IB for Llama~3.2 Vision), and its scorecards record the template used and every declared deviation.

All \cfWriteBlocks{} completed blocks pass checks (A), (B), (C), and (G). Determinism error and change at $\alpha=0$ are both zero, and fp32-versus-model logit differences are below \cfGateFpMax{}. In check (D), the Qwen3-VL, Gemma~3, MedGemma, InternVL, and Llama families exceed the 0.25-logit tolerance (up to \cfGateBatchMax{} logits for Gemma~3 12B). These are recorded as declared deviations and neutralized by fixed batch composition. Across all datasets, check (E) fails for the A/B templates of LLaVA-1.5-7B, LLaVA-Med, and LLaVA-Rad-7B and for the B-mapped templates of Qwen2.5-VL-3B, LLaVA-1.5-13B, CheXagent-2-3B, and CheXagent-8B. Check (E) also fails for the yes/no and A-mapped templates of Llama~3.2 Vision 11B. The affected cells are recorded as ineligible. Check (E) fails for every template of MAIRA-2 \citep{bannur2024maira} on NIH. The model answers ``No'' to every finding on all \cfSpecUngradedRows{} preflight images and ``A'' to every forced choice under both letter mappings. With no template carrying a graded answer, the block produces no outcomes. Two Gemma~3 checkpoints answer ``yes'' to most chest questions; Gemma~3 4B does so on \cfGemmaFourYesMin--\cfGemmaFourYesMax\% of rows (clean $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$). The answerability grade records this behavior. Three checkpoints (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, LLaVA-Med) answer at chance.

\subsection{Coverage and cost}
\label{app:cf-coverage}

Every checkpoint has a completed core write matrix and calibration pass on NIH ChestX-ray14, CheXpert Plus, and COCO. The campaign targets \cfCoveragePlannedWord{} modules per block. They are the write matrix, the calibration pass, the five additional templates, the steering strength sweep over $\alpha\in\{-0.5,-0.25,-0.1,+0.1,+0.5\}$, the two probe refits, the connector site, and the connector-site calibration. \cfCoverageBlocksAllSeven{} of \cfBlocks{} blocks carry all seven modules. A probe-graded block whose write matrix is ineligible or incomplete contributes readability alone. Each block's scorecard also reports the steering strength range of $O_q$ over $\alpha\in[-0.5,0.5]$, its standard deviation over the three fit seeds, ownership at the connector site, and the wording and mapping contrasts of $O_q$ across the six templates. The partial block is Qwen2.5-VL-72B on CheXpert Plus, which lacks the additional templates and the connector site. The campaign used about 3,000 A100-hours; a chest block of a 7B checkpoint takes about ten.

\subsection{What each analysis scores its writes against}
\label{app:cf-ledger}

Unless stated otherwise, an analysis uses the write matrix's rule. The own write must exceed the 95th percentile of the 119-direction random family and the absolute sham, and the verdict compares the own write with each competitor's on the same rows. \cfLedgerFullReference{} of the \cfLedgerAnalyses{} graded analyses (\cfLedgerCells{} cells) keep this reference, and \cfLedgerMaxT{} keep this rule. The exceptions are the semantic endpoints, the answer direction under held-out templates, and the logit-scale grade. The semantic endpoints take the maximum of \cfSemLedgerRandom{} random directions of their own. The answer direction uses the random 95th percentile in \cfAnsdirtRefPairs{} of its \cfAnsdirtLedgerPairs{} (cell, template) pairs and the sham alone in the rest. The logit-scale grade applies the same rule to the margin-scale ownership contrast. The probe refits, alternative constructions, displacement lifts, and expert-label refits (\cfLedgerSeedShamCells{} cells) take the sham of the seed-0 logistic normal.

\paragraph{Steering strength and site.} The steering strength sweep ran on \cfDoseBlocksNih{} NIH and \cfDoseBlocksCoco{} COCO blocks. On COCO, the ownership margins span $\cfCocoMarginMin$--$\cfCocoMarginMax$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families. \paragraph{Alternative directions.} Besides the logistic normal, we write four constructions per concept (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}): the difference of class means, the Haufe pattern, the normal orthogonalized against the other five normals, and the normal refitted on features residualized against the other five labels. A classifier coefficient lifts by Equation~\ref{eq:lift}. A difference of means or a pattern is instead a displacement $\boldsymbol u$ in the projected, train-scaled space, and its minimum-norm preimage gives the displacement lift
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat\wvec&\propto R(R^{\top}R)^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol s)\,\boldsymbol u,\\
\text{so that}\quad R^{\top}\hat\wvec&\propto\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol s)\,\boldsymbol u .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:displacement-lift}
\end{equation}
The two lifts differ because $R^{\top}R$ is not a multiple of the identity (Appendix~\ref{app:repro-probe}). The difference-of-means and pattern directions lie at a median absolute raw model-space cosine of $\cfAltdirCosMin$ to $\cfAltdirCosMax$ from the logistic normal.

\paragraph{Token weights and competitors.} A token-weighted write replaces the uniform steering strength of Equation~\ref{eq:intervention} with weights $\omega_t$ that average one over the $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ consumed tokens,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hvec'_{it}&=\hvec_{it}+\alpha\,\omega_t\lVert\hvec_{it}\rVert_2\,\hat\wvec_c,\\
\omega_t&=\begin{cases}
n_{\mathrm{tok}}\,e^{z_t}\big/\sum_{t'}e^{z_{t'}}, & \text{softmax,}\\
4\cdot\mathbf 1[\,z_t\in\text{top quarter}\,], & \text{top quarter,}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:token-weights}
\end{equation}
where $z_t$ is the probe's score of token $t$. The softmax variant keeps the uniform write's L1 steering strength and the top-quarter variant doubles its Frobenius norm. The extended competitor family, scored across \cfExtcompBlocks{} blocks, adds a direction for every other label with at least 100 known positives and 100 known negatives (NIH: Consolidation, Edema, Infiltration; CheXpert: Enlarged Cardiomediastinum, Fracture, Lung Lesion, Lung Opacity, Pneumonia, Support Devices). These directions are fitted and written like the protocol normals, and a cell is then owned only if its write also beats every added direction.

\paragraph{Crossed tower and reader.} We load the vision tower of Gemma~3 4B (G) behind the connector and language model of MedGemma 4B (M), and the reverse. All \cfSwapTensors{} weight tensors of the loaded tower equal the donor's bit for bit, and none of the \cfSwapOutside{} tensors outside it changes. Directions travel with their tower. Changing the tower therefore changes the representation and its six directions together, whereas changing the reader changes only the connector and the language model. All \cfSwapCombos{} combinations are graded on the same \cfSwapRows{} test rows of the block, at the primary steering strength and template, against the 119-direction random family and the sham of the tower in use. The two native combinations reproduce the checkpoints' own grades on this smaller cohort. Across the \cfSwapNativeCells{} native cells, the ownership contrast moves by at most \cfSwapNativeMaxDO{}, and \cfSwapNativeLostReference{} of the \cfSwapNativeOwnedFull{} owned cells no longer clear the steering reference. Writing $Y_q(g/h)$ for a quantity of question $q$ with tower $g$ and reader $h$, the mean reader and tower effects are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{\text{reader}}&=\frac{1}{12}\sum_{g}\sum_{q}\bigl|Y_q(g/\mathrm G)-Y_q(g/\mathrm M)\bigr|,\\
\delta_{\text{tower}}&=\frac{1}{12}\sum_{h}\sum_{q}\bigl|Y_q(\mathrm G/h)-Y_q(\mathrm M/h)\bigr|,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:crossover}
\end{equation}
for $Y_q\in\{W_{q,q},\,O_q,\,M_q\}$, where $M_q=W_{q,q}-\max(\max_{d\neq q}W_{q,d},\,p_{95},\,|W^{\mathrm{sham}}_q|)$ is the own write's margin over the whole reference family, with $p_{95}$ the random 95th percentile and $W^{\mathrm{sham}}_q$ the sham write.

\paragraph{Identical-tensor replay.} Checkpoints of one family share a vision tower, but two readers' inputs agree only up to bf16 rounding. The replay module stores the selected vision layer's token tensors once from the group's source checkpoint. The module feeds them to every reader of the group in place of that reader's own tower output, so all readers consume the same bits. Before the replacement it measures the drift, the largest absolute difference between the reader's own tower output and the stored tensor on the same rows. In \cfReplayDriftZero{} of the \cfReplayBlocks{} replays that output already matches the stored tensor bit for bit. The exception is LLaVA-Med, whose fine-tuned tower departs by up to \cfReplayDriftMax{} against a mean absolute tensor entry of \cfReplayTensorMin{}--\cfReplayTensorMax{}.

A reader that replays its own block's tensors tests the replacement path itself. Across the \cfReplaySelf{} such replays, no verdict and no ownership grade changes, \cfReplaySelfExact{} reproduce the block's grade exactly, and the third differs by at most $\cfReplayMaxDWSelf$ in a write magnitude. Across all \cfReplayBlocks{} replays, the change is distinguishable from zero in \cfReplayMoved{} of \cfReplayCells{} concept cells. The largest change in a write magnitude is $\cfReplayMaxDW$, and \cfReplayVerdictChanges{} verdicts and \cfReplayOwnedChanges{} ownership grades change. For the \cfReplayPairs{} reader pairs, the mean absolute difference between the two readers' own-question writes is \cfReplayPairOwnMin{}--\cfReplayPairOwnMax{} when each reads its own tower, and feeding both the stored tensors changes that difference by at most \cfReplayChangeMax{}, or \cfReplayRelMax\% of its size.

\subsection{The answer direction}
\label{app:cf-ansdir}

\paragraph{Fitting and writing.} For question $q$, let $m_{iq}=\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ be the clean answer margin of training image $i$ on the block's primary template, and $\boldsymbol x_i$ its projected, train-scaled features. The answer direction $a_q$ lifts, by Equation~\ref{eq:lift}, the ridge coefficients
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\gamma_q=\arg\min_{\gamma_0,\boldsymbol\gamma}\;\sum_{i}\bigl(m_{iq}-\gamma_0-\boldsymbol x_i^{\top}\boldsymbol\gamma\bigr)^2+\lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\gamma\rVert_2^2 ,
\label{eq:answer-direction}
\end{equation}
fitted on the first 3{,}000 training rows in the manifest's label-blind order, with $\lambda\in\{0.1,1,10,100\}$ chosen by five-fold cross-validated $R^2$ over patients or images. We write $a_q$ at the same steering strength against the write matrix's random family and a sham of the $a_q$ under test, with the other five $a_d$ as competitors. Under the five held-out templates, $a_q$ is written unchanged against each template's own clean baseline and sham.

\paragraph{Where it is owned.} The answer direction beats the strongest logistic competitor in \cfAnsChestBeats{} chest cells (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}). Over all held-out (cell, template) pairs it is owned in \cfAnsdirtChestOwnedPairs{} of \cfAnsdirtChestPairsAll{} chest and \cfAnsdirtCocoOwnedPairs{} of \cfAnsdirtCocoPairsAll{} COCO pairs, and it stays owned in \cfAnsdirtCocoKept{} of \cfAnsdirtCocoKeptN{} COCO pairs owned under the primary template. In Qwen2.5-VL-7B on NIH, the Effusion answer direction reaches $O_q=\cfAnsQwenEffO$ with an own write of $\cfAnsQwenEffW$.

\paragraph{What it reads.} In Qwen2.5-VL-7B on NIH (Table~\ref{tab:cf-validation}), the answer direction reaches an AUROC of $\cfValAurocAnsQwenNih$ against the label and $\cfValAurocAnsCleanQwenNih$ against the clean answer; the probe reaches $\cfValAurocProbeQwenNih$ and $\cfValAurocProbeCleanQwenNih$. Cosines depend on the inner product chosen for the representation \citep{park2024linear}. Besides the raw cosine we therefore report, for two coefficient vectors $\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\beta'$ in the projected space,
\begin{equation}
\cos_\Sigma(\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\beta')=\frac{\boldsymbol\beta^{\top}\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta'}{\sqrt{\boldsymbol\beta^{\top}\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta\;\boldsymbol\beta'^{\top}\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta'}},
\label{eq:whitened-cos}
\end{equation}
with $\Sigma$ the covariance of the training features. This is the correlation over training images between the two vectors' scores. Between answer and probe coefficients, the median raw cosine is $\cfAnsCosQwenMin$--$\cfAnsCosQwenMax$ on the chest blocks of Qwen2.5-VL-7B, $\cfAnsCosLingMin$--$\cfAnsCosLingMax$ on those of Lingshu 32B, and $\cfAnsCosCocoMin$--$\cfAnsCosCocoMax$ on COCO. The median whitened cosine is $\cfValCosSigmaChestMin$--$\cfValCosSigmaChestMax$ on the chest sets and $\cfValCosSigmaCoco$ on COCO. The two metrics differ most for Lingshu 32B on CheXpert ($\cfValCosRawLingChex$ raw, $\cfValCosSigmaLingChex$ whitened).

The column selectivity
\begin{equation}
\kappa_d=\frac{W_{d,d}}{\sum_q\lvert W_{q,d}\rvert}
\label{eq:column-selectivity}
\end{equation}
equals one when a write moves only its own answer. Over the \cfAnsColSelChestN{} chest cells its median is $\cfAnsColSelChest$ for answer directions and $\cfAnsColSelLabelChest$ for label directions, against $\cfAnsColSelCoco$ and $\cfAnsColSelLabelCoco$ over \cfAnsColSelCocoN{} COCO cells. Over all cells it is \cfValColSelNih{} on NIH, \cfValColSelChex{} on CheXpert, and \cfValColSelCoco{} on COCO. On the chest sets, both directions move the other five answers along with their own.

\paragraph{Semantic endpoints.} Four endpoints keep the write and change what is read after it. They are the negated question, whose effect must take the opposite sign; the forced choice between the finding and its strongest competitor, asked in both orders so that a position preference shows as a gap; and the log-probability of the finding word in a report continuation. Each carries its own clean baseline, sham, and \cfSemLedgerRandom{} random directions. With $E_q$ the signed endpoint effect of the own write, a cell meets the reference when
\begin{equation}
E_q>\max\Bigl(0,\;\max_{k}E^{\mathrm{rand}}_{k},\;\bigl|E^{\mathrm{sham}}_q\bigr|\Bigr),
\label{eq:endpoint-reference}
\end{equation}
the inner maximum running over the \cfSemLedgerRandom{} random directions, and it is owned when it also leads all $6\times5$ comparisons with the competitors.

Across \cfSemBlocks{} blocks, the negated question moves opposite to the affirmative one in \cfSemNegOpposite{} of \cfSemNegN{} concept--block pairs. The forced choice is owned in both orders in \cfSemForcedOwned{} of \cfSemForcedN{} pairs (mean order gap \cfSemGapMin{}--\cfSemGapMax{}), and the report continuation is owned in \cfSemReportOwned{} of \cfSemReportCells{} cells (Table~\ref{tab:cf-semend}). Adding the ownership contrast to a regression of the endpoint effect on write magnitude, probe selectivity, and clean-answer AUROC changes the held-out $R^2$ by $\cfSemCvLocoDelta$ when one concept is left out and $\cfSemCvLoboDelta$ when one of \cfSemCvBlocks{} blocks is left out. Permuting ownership within blocks \cfSemCvPerms{} times gives $p=\cfSemCvLocoP$ and $p=\cfSemCvLoboP$. The predictors vary by concept, so each block contributes an effective sample of six.

\subsection{Difficulty}
\label{app:cf-difficulty}

\paragraph{Fine-grained objects.} Six further COCO categories test whether ownership on COCO depends on large, easy objects. A rule stated in full in Appendix~\ref{app:repro-protocol} picks them from the label counts and mean instance areas of the fixed cohorts alone. The protocol file registers the rule and the two matching windows before the module's first outcome. The categories are fitted, written, and graded like the six easy objects, as their own family in the same blocks and rows. Their clean-answer AUROC is measured on the same calibration rows. Within a checkpoint the owned counts of fine-grained and easy objects differ by $\cfFgSameDiffMin$ to $\cfFgSameDiffMax$ of six, and median probe selectivity is \cfFgFineSel{} for the fine-grained objects against \cfFgEasySameSel{} for the easy ones.

\paragraph{Difficulty matching.} Each chest cell $c$ is paired with the natural-image cells $\mathcal P(c)$ whose matching variables lie within \cfFgWindow{} of its own. With $o\in\{0,1\}$ a cell's owned indicator, $\mathcal C$ the chest cells with at least one partner, and $\mathcal P=\bigcup_{c\in\mathcal C}\mathcal P(c)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\text{pool}}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal C|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}o_c-\frac{1}{|\mathcal P|}\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}o_p,\\
\Delta_{\text{match}}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal C|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\Bigl(o_c-\frac{1}{|\mathcal P(c)|}\sum_{p\in\mathcal P(c)}o_p\Bigr);
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}
$\Delta_{\text{pool}}$ compares the two matched pools, and $\Delta_{\text{match}}$ weights every chest cell equally, however many partners it has. Neither can be derived from the other. Chest cells without a partner leave the matching, which the stratified comparison below avoids. Matched on clean-answer AUROC, $\Delta_{\text{match}}$ shrinks to $\cfFgMatchAnsDiff$ (Table~\ref{tab:cf-matching}). Matched on both variables, it is $\cfFgMatchBothDiff$ on the \cfFgMatchBothN{} of \cfFgMatchBothCells{} chest cells that keep a partner. Both values are near zero because of support. Below a clean-answer AUROC of \cfStratTopEdge{} the grid holds 263 chest cells but only 24 natural-image cells, 2 of them owned, so most chest cells are matched to the same few hard objects, which are rarely owned. The stratified comparison is therefore the primary difficulty control: its top bin is the only one populated in both domains, and there the difference is $\cfStratCvnDiff$ (95\% interval $[\cfStratCvnLo,\cfStratCvnHi]$). The stratified comparison of Section~\ref{sec:coco} keeps every cell instead and bins both pools by clean-answer AUROC at the edges \cfStratEdges{}. The \cfStratChestTopN{} chest cells of the top bin are all CheXpert Plus cells, because no NIH ChestX-ray14 cell reaches \cfStratTopEdge{}.

\section{Pilot study: registered protocol}
\label{app:seed}

The two-model study that motivated RAW registered the procedures below; Appendix~\ref{app:seed-results} reports its results. The study uses the construction of Section~\ref{sec:framework}, with the differences stated here.

\paragraph{Data, models, and reading.}
We use NIH ChestX-ray14 \citep{wang2017chestxray}, whose findings are mined from radiology reports. A deterministic hash of patient identity assigns rows to disjoint train, validation, and test partitions: 18,212 training images and 5,343 test images from 1,659 test patients. The three cells pair LLaVA-1.5-7B \citep{liu2024improved} with Effusion and Edema, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B \citep{bai2025qwen25vl} with Effusion, at the final visual block consumed by the connector or merger. Hook tests confirm that this block lies on the downstream path, that $\alpha=0$ is a bitwise no-op, and that nonzero strengths change downstream logits. Projection, scaling, and probe follow Section~\ref{sec:directions} with seed 0. Selectivity is Equation~\ref{eq:selectivity} on test rows, with 20 control seeds mapping the 39 recurring input types (AP view, sex, and age decade) to random labels. Selectivity compares the concept with nuisance-type label maps of the same capacity, and the write then tests whether the model uses the concept. Prompts appear in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\paragraph{Write and strengths.}
The write is Equation~\ref{eq:intervention} along the lifted normal of Equation~\ref{eq:lift}, with every entry of $\boldsymbol s$ floored at $10^{-8}$. The endpoint is the paired mean change in $P(\mathrm{yes})$ from the common unsteered baseline (Section~\ref{sec:ownership}). Original cells use the strengths $\{-1,-0.5,-0.25,-0.1,0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1\}$ on 200 held-out test images. The controls are 20 isotropic random unit directions, one coordinate-permutation sham, and the fixed alternative clinical normals. At the six controlled strengths $\mathcal A=\{\pm1,\pm0.5,\pm0.25\}$, each control receives the same signed steering strength on the same rows. Monotonicity in steering strength over $[-0.5,0.5]$ is reported descriptively.

\paragraph{Original-cell decision rule.}
With $\Delta_c(\alpha)$ the target direction's paired mean change at steering strength $\alpha$, the primary effect and the steering strength that attains it are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^\star_c&=\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}\operatorname{sign}(\alpha)\,\Delta_c(\alpha),\\
\alpha^\star&=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}\operatorname{sign}(\alpha)\,\Delta_c(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:seed-rule}
\end{equation}
A cell clears the generic gate when $\Delta^\star_c$ exceeds the largest absolute sham effect over $\mathcal A$ and the 95th percentile of the 20 random directions' changes at $\alpha^\star$, each change multiplied by $\operatorname{sign}(\alpha^\star)$. Because the random directions are evaluated at $\alpha^\star$ alone, the gate is a registered reference with no correction across steering strengths.

\paragraph{Direction-specificity supplement.}
The Nodule response motivated a separately registered supplement at the locked Qwen steering strength $\alpha=+0.25$. The supplement uses 400 ChestX-ray14 test patients absent from the original cohort, with one row per patient chosen by label-blind hash. The supplement estimates the Effusion row of the write matrix (Section~\ref{sec:ownership}). The competitors are the five other pre-existing Qwen normals (Atelectasis, Pneumothorax, Cardiomegaly, Mass, and Nodule), fitted like Effusion, plus 20 random directions and the sham. This gives 28 configurations and 11,200 per-image outcomes. Direction specificity requires an Effusion effect above the random 95th percentile and the absolute sham, and $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}$ (Equation~\ref{eq:margin}) above zero. Adding competitors can only lower $O_q$. A positive advantage therefore holds for the family tested, and a negative one does not require an exhaustive family.

\paragraph{Causal-ownership matrix.}
A prospective grid crosses six Qwen directions (Effusion, Atelectasis, Pneumothorax, Cardiomegaly, Mass, and Nodule) with their six questions. The grid uses 400 new patients at $\alpha=+0.25$, with 119 random directions and a sham. Question $q$ is owned when $W_{q,q}$ exceeds every other clinical direction, every random direction, and the absolute sham. A separate family of 12 statistics tests shared aliasing. For each direction, these are its signed mean effect on its five off-diagonal questions and its mean excess over the matching diagonals on those questions.

\paragraph{Answer-encoding crossover and Mass confirmation.} A registered crossover uses 200 patients of the causal-ownership cohort, with the other 200 as a patient-disjoint calibration set. The crossover pairs the original yes/no prompt with A-present and B-present prompts, which exchange the finding-to-letter mapping. The writes use $\alpha=\pm0.25$ with six clinical directions, 20 random directions, and a sham. A question enters only if both coded mappings have clean-answer AUROC lower bounds above 0.5 with at least ten patients per class. An independent confirmation then tests Mass on 200 patients absent from every earlier Qwen cohort. The confirmation crosses two wordings (\textit{show}, \textit{is}) with explicit yes/no, A-present, and B-present instructions at $\alpha=+0.25$, with 119 random directions, a sham, and 20 clinical comparisons. Confirmation requires a positive margin against every competitor and Mass effects above every random effect and the absolute sham, across all four coded cells or both original-wording cells.

\subsection{A non-generic write effect reproduces}
\label{sec:qwen-replication}

The first finding is a successful intervention. Qwen Effusion reproduces beyond random and sham controls, and only the clinical alternatives tested afterwards change its attribution.

Controlled decodability is evaluated at the final visual block each connector consumes: the penultimate CLIP encoder layer for LLaVA and the last of Qwen's 32 visual blocks. The control AUROC is the mean over the 20 recurring-type random-label maps.

Qwen Effusion first satisfies the reader test. Its probe reaches AUROC \cfSeedDecQwenEffAuroc{} against a control AUROC of \cfSeedDecQwenEffCtrl{}, giving a controlled selectivity of \cfSeedDecQwenEffSel{}. LLaVA-1.5-7B reads both of its concepts at its selected vision layer, against a control AUROC of \cfSeedDecLlavaEffCtrl{}. Effusion reaches AUROC \cfSeedDecLlavaEffAuroc{} with selectivity \cfSeedDecLlavaEffSel{}, and Edema reaches AUROC \cfSeedDecLlavaEdemaAuroc{} with selectivity \cfSeedDecLlavaEdemaSel{}. For the original-cohort interventions, we take the maximum concept-consistent effect over six fixed nonzero strengths. In the original 200-image cohort, Qwen's maximum concept-consistent change is 0.2343 at $\alpha=+0.25$. This exceeds the random-direction 95th percentile of 0.0963 and the maximum absolute sham effect of 0.0852, a strong case for the write direction.

The response at the locked steering strength $\alpha=+0.25$ reproduces on the patient-disjoint 400-patient cohort. Effusion changes mean $P(\mathrm{yes})$ by 0.1945, again above the random-direction 95th percentile of 0.0878 and absolute sham effect of 0.0157. None of these patients appears in the original intervention cohort, and the steering strength was not reselected. The result supports a large, reproducible, non-generic effect within ChestX-ray14.


\subsection{Semantic competition changes attribution, not efficacy}
\label{sec:specificity-results}

The fixed clinical family is a harder identification test than non-semantic controls. Nodule changes the Effusion answer by 0.2519, the largest point estimate among the five alternatives. The Effusion ownership contrast $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}$ is $-0.0573$. The registered fixed-family endpoint takes the maximum over the five alternatives on the same patients. The contrast is thus a direct comparison between measured effects, with no tolerance attached. Descriptively, all five alternative clinical normals move the Effusion endpoint in the same positive direction. Effusion exceeds four of them by 0.1172--0.1778, and only Nodule has a larger point estimate. This shared-sign pattern motivates an answer-sensitive-alias hypothesis but cannot distinguish it from question-specific ownership.

Qwen Effusion thus remains a reproducible direction that changes the answer beyond random and sham perturbations, but its specific semantic attribution fails. The familywise endpoint establishes that at least one fixed clinical alternative is stronger. Nodule is the point-estimate maximizer, and the familywise test does not separately identify it as the semantic owner.

\section{Questions, Scores, and Notation}
\label{app:protocol}

\paragraph{Templates and score.} Each concept is asked in six templates, which cross two wordings with three answer interfaces. The wordings are \emph{is there} (I) and \emph{does the image show} (W); the interfaces are yes/no (Y), A-present (A), and B-present (B). A template is named by its wording and its interface, and the primary template is IY. At the first answer position, $\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}$ and $\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ are the maximum log probabilities among the valid single-token case and leading-space variants of each answer. The registered score is $P(\mathrm{yes})=\sigma(\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}})$, where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. Taking the best valid variant for each answer makes the score insensitive to which capitalization or leading-space form receives the highest probability. The score measures the relative support for yes versus no at the first answer position; it does not assess a generated report. Questions are the rows of $W$ and directions are its columns. Changing a direction therefore never changes the question being scored.

\section{Reproducibility specification}
\label{app:repro}

This appendix specifies each family's selected vision layer, the consumed-token mask, the projection and probe, the control constructions, the cohort roles, and the protocol record at the registered protocol's level of detail. The released code implements each component.

\subsection{Consumed sites per family}
\label{app:repro-sites}

The selected vision layer is the last visual block that feeds the consumer. The connector site is the consumer's output that enters the language model. We identify the block from each checkpoint's own configuration when loading the checkpoint. Each block's preflight record stores the identified block, its hidden dimension, its consumer, and any branch that bypasses it.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item Qwen2.5-VL, Lingshu, and Qwen3-VL: the merger reads the last block of the vision transformer, which has 32 blocks in Qwen2.5-VL and Lingshu, 24 in Qwen3-VL 4B, and 27 in Qwen3-VL 8B and 32B. A Qwen2.5-VL or Lingshu image contributes 576 patch tokens, which the merger joins in $2\times2$ groups into 144; a Qwen3-VL image contributes at most 1{,}024. The Qwen3-VL DeepStack mergers, which read earlier blocks, are not written.
\item Gemma~3 and MedGemma: the projector reads the last of the 27 SigLIP encoder layers through the tower's final layer normalization, and consumes all 4{,}096 patch tokens of the $896\times896$ image.
\item InternVL3.5: the projector reads the last InternViT encoder layer (of 24 at 8B and 14B, and of 45 at 38B). The tower's final normalization is an identity in the released configuration. An image contributes 1{,}024 patch tokens from a single $448\times448$ tile.
\item LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-Med: the projector reads the penultimate of the 24 CLIP encoder layers, and the final layer is computed and discarded. An image contributes 576 patch tokens.
\item Llama~3.2 Vision: the projector input concatenates the last of the eight global-encoder layers with five intermediate layers of the local encoder, which bypass the selected vision layer. An image contributes 1{,}601 tokens from its one real $560\times560$ tile: the CLS token and 1{,}600 patches.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:cf-models} lists the consumed-token count of every checkpoint.

\subsection{The consumed-token mask}
\label{app:repro-mask}

The write in Equation~\ref{eq:intervention} changes only the consumed tokens at the positions the consumer reads at the site. We derive these positions for each batch from that batch's processor outputs:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item Qwen2.5-VL, Lingshu, and Qwen3-VL: the tokens of all images in a batch form one sequence. Each image occupies one contiguous span whose bounds follow from its patch grid. At the connector site the span is a quarter as long, one merged token per $2\times2$ patch group.
\item Gemma~3 and MedGemma: all 4{,}096 patch tokens; the tower produces no CLS token. The number of written tokens must equal the number of image placeholder tokens in the language-model input.
\item InternVL3.5: the patch tokens of the single $448\times448$ tile. The CLS token, which the consumer drops, is excluded. The number of written tokens must equal the number of image placeholder tokens.
\item LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-Med: the 576 patch tokens. The CLS token, which the checkpoint's feature selection drops, is excluded.
\item Llama~3.2 Vision: the 1{,}601 tokens of the one real $560\times560$ tile (CLS and 1{,}600 patches). The seven padding tokens of each tile and the three padding tiles are excluded, matching the model's own aspect-ratio and cross-attention masks.
\end{itemize}

Scoring stops with an error and no fallback when an image span does not exactly cover the site, a mask length differs from the token count at the site, an image has no consumed token, or one forward pass reaches the site more than once.

Preflight check (C) verifies the mask. The check captures the site tensor on a clean pass and a pass steered at $\alpha=+0.25$ along the first concept direction. The check then reports the maximum absolute difference outside the mask and the maximum absolute changes in the consumer's output and answer logits. Across all \cfBlocks{} blocks, the change outside the consumed tokens is $0$ at both sites. The consumer's output and answer logits both change. The same records show a bitwise-identical repeated clean pass (check A) and a bitwise-identical $\alpha=0$ pass (check B) for every block.

\subsection{Projection, lift, scaler, and probe}
\label{app:repro-probe}

We construct the projection $R\in\R^{D\times512}$ by drawing standard normal variates once from a PCG64 generator seeded with $0$, dividing by $\sqrt{512}$, and storing the result in float32. Its entries are independent $\mathcal{N}(0,1/512)$ variates. The seed is $0$ for every checkpoint, dataset, and site. $D$ is the family's hidden dimension. We neither normalize nor orthogonalize the columns of $R$. The Gram matrix $R^{\top}R/D$ has full rank 512 in all \cfAltdirdBlocks{} blocks that carry the displacement module, with eigenvalues $\cfAltdirdEigMin$--$\cfAltdirdEigMax$ and condition number \cfAltdirdCondMin{}--\cfAltdirdCondMax{}.

Projecting the token-mean read vector $\hvec_i$ gives features $R^{\top}\hvec_i$. A featurewise standardizer fitted only on training rows supplies the mean $\boldsymbol\mu$ and scale $\boldsymbol s$. Every row is standardized as $(R^{\top}\hvec-\boldsymbol\mu)\oslash\max(\boldsymbol s,10^{-8})$, where $\oslash$ and $\max$ act elementwise. Each concept probe is an $\ell_2$-regularized logistic regression with inverse regularization strength $C=1$. We fit each probe by L-BFGS for at most 2{,}000 iterations with unweighted classes and seed $0$, using training rows with a known label for that concept. For each of the two refit seeds, we resample training units with replacement, retaining all rows of each sampled unit at its multiplicity. We then refit the scaler and six probes using the same projection. The random family, shams, and control probes remain at seed 0.

\subsection{Control constructions}
\label{app:repro-controls}

\paragraph{Random directions.} We draw one $119\times D$ standard-normal matrix using a PCG64 generator seeded with $0$ and normalize each row to unit $\ell_2$ norm. Every question in the block uses the same 119 directions.

\paragraph{Coordinate-permutation sham.} Immediately after the random draw, the same generator produces one permutation of $\{0,\dots,D{-}1\}$ per concept, in protocol concept order. We form concept $c$'s sham by rearranging the coordinates of $\hat\wvec_c$ with concept $c$'s own permutation. This preserves the direction's coordinate multiset and unit norm. We write each question with its own sham.

\paragraph{Matched random-label probes.} Each row has a type: view $\times$ sex $\times$ age decade on the chest sets, and aspect-ratio $\times$ area buckets on COCO. For control seed $k\in\{0,\dots,19\}$, we traverse the types in sorted order and use a PCG64 generator seeded with $k$ to draw one label per type uniformly from $\{0,1\}$. If all types receive the same label, we flip the first. Every row of a type receives that type's label. We give each control probe the concept probe's capacity and nuisance structure by fitting it on the same training rows and known-label subset with the same hyperparameters. A block fits control probes when its training rows span at least eight types. Selectivity is Equation~\ref{eq:selectivity}. A cell is graded for readability when the real AUROC is finite and at least ten of the twenty control AUROCs are finite.

\subsection{Cohort roles}
\label{app:repro-cohorts}

Every chest image is a frontal study. Only the NIH training role holds more than one row per unit. That role takes every study of each training patient, \cfCohortNihTrainRows{} studies from \cfCohortNihTrainUnits{} patients. Of these patients, \cfCohortNihTrainMulti{} contribute more than one study, and one contributes \cfCohortNihTrainMax{}. Every other role of every dataset holds one row per unit, and no unit appears in two roles of a dataset. We use the calibration role only to determine readability and answerability. We neither fit probes nor score writes on it. Every write uses the test role.

\subsection{Protocol amendments}
\label{app:repro-protocol}

Every analysis the paper reports beyond the \cfCoveragePlannedWord{} planned modules was added to the protocol file as a dated amendment after the first wave. Each amendment precedes the first result of the module it registers, and within each entry the rows, references, and decision rule come before any result.


\FloatBarrier

\paragraph{The fine-grained category selection rule.} The protocol file states in full the rule that picks the six fine-grained COCO categories, and the rule depends on the labels alone. A category is eligible when it meets three conditions. Its training positives are at least as many as those of the weakest of the six existing concepts. The rule uses only the label counts and instance areas of the fixed cohorts; no probe, answer, or write enters it. The protocol file records the rule under the date of its amendment, and the module's first result postdates it. The six categories and the two matching windows were therefore fixed before any of them was scored.
