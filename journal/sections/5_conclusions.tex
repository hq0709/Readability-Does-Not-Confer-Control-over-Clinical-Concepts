\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Medical and medically fine-tuned vision-language models read and answer chest findings, but the direction that names a finding rarely controls the model's answer about it. Medical fine-tuning and chest-specific pretraining do not close this gap. Explanations and edits built on these directions can therefore present the radiologist with evidence for the wrong finding and change findings that no one intended to change. These models are not yet reliable sources of finding-level diagnostic evidence. Before clinical use, selective control should be demonstrated for each finding with a competitor- and off-target-aware evaluation such as RAW, and the radiologist should remain responsible for every finding in the report.
